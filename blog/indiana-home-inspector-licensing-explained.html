Is Indiana Home Inspector Licensing Required? What Credentials Actually Matter

February 28, 2026 · 3 min read

Unlike states such as Texas or Illinois, Indiana does not currently require home inspectors to hold a specific state-issued license. That surprises a lot of buyers when they first hear it — and it means the burden of vetting an inspector's actual qualifications falls more heavily on you than it would in a licensed state where a baseline is set for everyone.

This gap surprises even experienced buyers who've purchased homes in other states and assumed licensing worked the same way everywhere. It doesn't, and understanding that difference up front changes how you should be vetting an inspector before you ever hand over a deposit.

No statewide license — what that actually means

Because there's no Indiana licensing board setting a minimum bar for home inspectors, anyone can technically hang out a shingle and call themselves one. That doesn't mean quality is purely a coin flip — most serious inspectors in the Indianapolis market pursue independent certification anyway, since it's how they demonstrate credibility to buyers and agents — but it does mean the credentials-checking step that a license would otherwise handle for you needs to happen on your end instead.

The certifications worth looking for

Two national organizations set the bar in the absence of state licensing: InterNACHI (International Association of Certified Home Inspectors) and ASHI (American Society of Home Inspectors). Both require members to pass an exam, follow a defined standards of practice, and complete ongoing continuing education. An inspector who holds one of these certifications has voluntarily opted into a standard that Indiana law doesn't actually require — which tells you something meaningful about how seriously they take the work.

Insurance matters more than a license would

With no state licensing board to fall back on, insurance coverage becomes your main practical protection if something significant gets missed. Ask directly whether the inspector carries Errors & Omissions (E&O) and general liability insurance, and don't be shy about asking to see proof before you book. An inspector without E&O coverage has effectively no accountability if a major defect is missed — there's no license to revoke and no state bond to claim against if a dispute happens later.

What to ask instead of 'are you licensed?'

Since "are you licensed" isn't really the right question in Indiana, ask these instead: Which certification do you hold, and is it current? How many inspections have you completed? Do you carry E&O and general liability insurance, and can I see proof? Do you offer a sample report I can review before booking? These four questions do the job that a state license would otherwise be doing for you in a different state.

Bottom line

The absence of state licensing in Indiana puts more responsibility on buyers to vet who they're hiring — it's not a reason to skip an inspection or assume all inspectors are roughly equal, because in practice they're not. Key takeaways

  • Indiana does not require a state-issued license for home inspectors, unlike states such as Texas or Illinois.
  • InterNACHI and ASHI certification are the main quality signals to look for in the absence of state licensing.
  • E&O and general liability insurance matter more here than in licensed states, since there's no state board to fall back on.
  • Ask about certification, experience, insurance, and sample reports instead of asking whether an inspector is "licensed."
