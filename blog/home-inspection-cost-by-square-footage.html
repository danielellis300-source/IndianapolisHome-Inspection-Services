Home Inspection Cost by Home Size: What Indianapolis Buyers Should Budget

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Square footage is the single biggest driver of home inspection pricing — more than location, more than age, more than the number of bathrooms. Time on site scales with the amount of house there is to walk, photograph, and test, and most Indianapolis inspectors price accordingly. Here's roughly what to expect at each size tier in the local market, so you can budget accurately before you request a quote.

Under 1,200 sq ft — condos, townhomes, starter homes

Expect to pay $250–$325. Smaller footprints simply take less time to walk, and condos/townhomes often exclude exterior and roof inspection responsibilities that belong to an HOA rather than the individual unit owner. If you're buying a condo, confirm with your inspector exactly what's in scope — some HOA-maintained items (shared roofs, exterior siding, common-area mechanicals) may be excluded from the report entirely, which is normal but worth knowing in advance.

1,200–2,200 sq ft — the Indianapolis sweet spot

This is where most Marion County resale homes fall, and pricing typically runs $325–$425. This size band covers the classic Indianapolis bungalow, ranch, and two-story homes found in neighborhoods like Broad Ripple, Irvington, and Beech Grove, as well as comparable homes in Greenwood and Plainfield. Most inspections in this range take right around 2.5–3 hours on site.

2,200–3,200 sq ft — larger family homes

Budget $400–$500. This size is common in newer subdivisions across Fishers, Westfield, and Noblesville, where two-story homes with finished basements and three-car garages push both the square footage and the inspection time up. A finished basement in this range typically adds 30–45 minutes to the visit since it's inspected with the same rigor as the rest of the living space.

3,200+ sq ft — larger homes in Carmel and Zionsville

Expect $500–$700+. Beyond 3,200 sq ft, inspectors are often walking multiple HVAC zones, more extensive electrical service (sometimes 200-amp or higher), and finished lower levels, which adds real time to both the visit and the report itself. If the home has a pool, outbuilding, or guest suite, ask whether that's quoted separately — it usually is, and it's better to know before the invoice than after.

Basements and crawl spaces change the math

A finished basement adds livable square footage and typically adds cost accordingly, since it's inspected to the same standard as any above-grade room. An unfinished basement or crawl space doesn't add "square footage" in the traditional sense, but it does add inspection time — especially a crawl space that requires the inspector to physically enter and photograph framing, insulation, and moisture conditions, which is common given Indiana's clay soil and the moisture issues it tends to create underneath a home.

See our guide on crawl space and basement inspections for what that specific part of the visit involves and why it's worth the extra time rather than a quick glance from the hatch.

Ranges like these are useful for budgeting, but pricing shouldn't be a guessing game once you have an actual address and listing sheet in hand — tell us the square footage, age, and any known extras and we'll give you a firm number before you book, no surprise add-ons once we're on site.

Key takeaways

  • Under 1,200 sq ft: $250–$325. 1,200–2,200 sq ft: $325–$425. 2,200–3,200 sq ft: $400–$500. 3,200+ sq ft: $500–$700+.
  • Condos and townhomes often exclude HOA-maintained items like shared roofs, so confirm scope before booking.
  • A finished basement is inspected like any other living space and typically adds 30–45 minutes to the visit.
  • Pools, guest suites, and detached outbuildings are usually quoted separately from the base square-footage price.
