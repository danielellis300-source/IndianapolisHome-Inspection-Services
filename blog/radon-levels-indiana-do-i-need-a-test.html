Radon in Indiana: Do You Really Need a Test Before Buying?

February 16, 2026 · 3 min read

Unlike some states, Indiana doesn't legally require a radon test as part of a home sale. That leaves the decision entirely up to the buyer — and given the geology of central Indiana, it's a decision worth making deliberately rather than skipping just to save $150 on your inspection invoice.

Because testing is optional rather than mandatory, the decision often gets made by default — buyers simply don't think to ask, and sellers have no legal obligation to bring it up first. That default matters a lot in a Zone 1 state, since silence on the topic doesn't mean the risk isn't there.

Indiana has no mandatory radon testing law

There is no state statute requiring radon testing or disclosure as a condition of sale in Indiana. Some sellers voluntarily disclose a prior test result on the Seller's Disclosure form if one exists, but there's no legal obligation to test before listing a home in the first place. In practice, this means radon testing is entirely opt-in for buyers, and a meaningful share of Indianapolis-area homes are sold without ever being tested at all.

Why that matters here specifically

The EPA's radon zone map places most of central Indiana — including Marion, Hamilton, Boone, Hendricks, Johnson, and Hancock counties — in Zone 1, the highest radon potential category in the country. That doesn't mean every home tests high, but it means the baseline risk locally is well above the national average, which is exactly the scenario where skipping the test to save money is the wrong call.

Radon doesn't correlate with how the house looks

This is the detail that surprises most buyers: radon levels have essentially nothing to do with the home's age, condition, or price point. A beautifully renovated home in Carmel and a dated fixer-upper in Lawrence can both test high, because radon enters through soil gas and foundation cracks rather than through anything visible during a normal walkthrough. The only way to actually know is to run the 48-hour test.

What to do if you're buying without a test

If a seller's disclosure mentions a prior radon test, ask for the actual report, not just a verbal summary — and check the date carefully. Radon levels can change over time due to foundation settling, sump pump modifications, or grading changes made nearby. A test that's more than two or three years old shouldn't be treated as current information for your own decision-making.

Our recommendation

Add radon testing to every Indianapolis-area home purchase, full stop. At $125–$175 bundled with your inspection, it's inexpensive insurance against a health risk that's completely fixable — mitigation systems run about $1,000–$1,500 and are frequently negotiated as a seller-paid item when a high result comes back during the due diligence period. Key takeaways

  • Indiana has no state law requiring radon testing or disclosure as part of a home sale.
  • Marion, Hamilton, Boone, Hendricks, Johnson, and Hancock counties all fall in EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest-risk category.
  • Radon levels don't correlate with a home's age, price, or visible condition — testing is the only way to know.
  • A prior test result more than two or three years old shouldn't be treated as current information.
