Winterizing Your Indianapolis Home: A Room-by-Room Checklist

April 5, 2026 · 3 min read

Winterizing isn't one task — it's a dozen small ones spread across every part of the house, easy to miss if you're only thinking about the furnace. Here's a room-by-room pass to make sure nothing gets overlooked before Indiana's coldest months arrive in full force.

Unlike the broader fall maintenance checklist, this list focuses specifically on preventing frozen pipes and heat loss room by room, which is where most winter emergency calls actually originate. A weekend spent working through it room by room is cheap insurance against a burst pipe in January.

Working through this list room by room, rather than trying to remember everything at once, is the easiest way to make sure nothing gets skipped before the first real cold snap arrives.

Basement and crawl space

Insulate any exposed water pipes with foam sleeves, especially along exterior walls where freezing risk is highest during extended cold snaps. Check crawl space vents — some should be closed for winter depending on your home's specific ventilation design, so check what's actually recommended for your setup rather than assuming a blanket rule applies. Seal any obvious gaps where cold air is entering around the rim joist, since this is a common and often-overlooked source of heat loss.

Attic

Confirm insulation is at an adequate depth (R-38 to R-60 is typical for central Indiana's climate) and evenly distributed across the whole attic floor, with no bare or thin spots. Check that attic ventilation isn't blocked by insulation that's crept over the soffit vents — proper airflow helps prevent ice dams by keeping the roof deck temperature more consistent across the whole roof surface.

Kitchen and bathrooms

On the coldest nights of the year, open cabinet doors under sinks located on exterior walls to let warm room air reach the pipes inside, and let faucets drip slightly during extreme cold snaps to relieve pressure in case a freeze does occur anyway. This is a low-effort habit that prevents one of the most common and expensive winter plumbing failures homeowners deal with here.

Living areas

Reverse ceiling fans to run clockwise at low speed, which pushes warm air that's naturally risen to the ceiling back down into the room where people actually are. Check for drafts around windows and doors with a hand or a lit candle on a still day, and add weatherstripping or a door draft stopper wherever you find one — these small drafts add up to a noticeable amount on a monthly heating bill.

Garage

If your garage is attached and unheated, make sure the door between the garage and the interior of the house seals well against drafts. An uninsulated garage door combined with a poor seal at that interior door together can meaningfully drop the temperature of adjacent rooms all winter long. If any pipes run through the garage, insulate them the same way you would in the basement or crawl space.

Key takeaways

  • Insulate exposed pipes along exterior walls in the basement, crawl space, and garage before the first hard freeze.
  • Confirm attic insulation is at R-38 to R-60 and evenly distributed, with ventilation paths kept clear.
  • Let faucets drip and open cabinet doors under exterior-wall sinks during extreme cold snaps to prevent frozen pipes.
  • Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise at low speed to push risen warm air back down into living spaces.
