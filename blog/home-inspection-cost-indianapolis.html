How Much Does a Home Inspection Cost in Indianapolis? (2026 Price Guide)

July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

If you're buying a home in Indianapolis, the inspection is one of the smallest line items on your closing statement — and one of the highest-leverage. A $350 inspection can surface $15,000 in deferred roof or HVAC repairs before you're legally on the hook for them. Here's what inspections actually cost across the Indianapolis metro right now, what's included, and what moves the price up or down.

Typical Indianapolis home inspection pricing

A standard full home inspection in Indianapolis and Marion County generally runs $300 to $500, with most single-family homes between 1,500 and 2,800 square feet landing around $350–$425. Condos and smaller townhomes often come in under $300, while larger homes in suburbs like Carmel and Zionsville can run $450–$600+ once square footage, outbuildings, and add-on tests are factored in.

Pricing is almost always based on square footage and age, not a flat rate — a 1,000 sq ft bungalow in Fountain Square and a 1,000 sq ft home in Fishers should cost roughly the same, all else equal. If a quote seems disconnected from the home's actual size, ask what it's based on before booking.

What's included in a standard inspection fee

The base fee covers a full visual inspection of the roof, attic and insulation, structural components, foundation, exterior grading and drainage, HVAC, plumbing, electrical panel and wiring, and every interior room, plus a same-day digital report with photos. A typical visit runs 2–4 hours on site, with the written report usually delivered the same evening.

It does not automatically include radon testing, sewer scope, mold testing, or well/septic evaluation — those are priced separately because they require additional equipment, lab time, or a longer on-site window. Always ask for the full scope, not just the base number, before comparing quotes between inspectors.

Common add-on tests and their cost

  • Radon testing: typically $125–$175, run over a continuous 48-hour period during the inspection
  • Sewer scope inspection: typically $125–$225 depending on line length and access
  • Mold inspection/testing: $175–$400 depending on the number of samples taken
  • Well and septic inspection: $150–$350, common in outer-ring suburbs like Mooresville and Lebanon that aren't on municipal service

Bundling add-ons with your main inspection is almost always cheaper than scheduling a separate visit later, since the inspector is already on site and equipment doesn't need a second trip. Ask for an all-in quote up front rather than pricing each item separately after the fact.

What makes Indianapolis pricing go up

Four things reliably push the quote higher: square footage over 3,000 sq ft, a crawl space or unfinished basement that needs to be physically entered rather than viewed from a hatch, homes built before 1978 (which may trigger extra attention to older wiring, plumbing, and lead-based paint discussion), and detached structures like a barn, pole building, or guest house that get added to the scope.

Rural properties around Mooresville or Lebanon with a private well and septic system also tend to run higher, since each of those systems is essentially its own mini-inspection with its own equipment and checklist. Spring and early summer, the busiest buying season locally, can also mean less flexibility on same-week scheduling — booking a day or two earlier than you think you need to rarely hurts.

Is the cheapest inspector actually the best deal?

Rarely. A rock-bottom price is often a sign of a rushed inspection, an inspector who isn't carrying full E&O and general liability insurance, or a report that skips photos entirely. In a $300,000+ purchase, the difference between a $275 inspector and a $425 inspector is a rounding error — but the difference between a thorough report and a thin one can cost you tens of thousands after closing.

A useful gut check: ask what a quote excludes, not just what it includes. Inspectors racing to underbid competitors often do it by trimming time on site, not by being more efficient. See our guide on choosing a home inspector in Indianapolis for the specific red flags to watch for before you book.

Key takeaways

  • A standard Indianapolis home inspection runs $300–$500, with most 1,500–2,800 sq ft homes landing around $350–$425.
  • Radon, sewer scope, mold testing, and well/septic evaluation are separate add-ons, not automatically included in the base fee.
  • Homes over 3,000 sq ft, older properties, and rural homes with a private well and septic tend to cost more to inspect.
  • The cheapest quote in town is rarely the best value once you factor in insurance coverage and report quality.
