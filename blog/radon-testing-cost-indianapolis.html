Radon Testing Cost in Indianapolis: What to Expect and Why It's Worth It

January 17, 2026 · 3 min read

Radon is invisible, odorless, and the second-leading cause of lung cancer in the U.S. after smoking — and most of central Indiana sits in an EPA zone where elevated levels are common. Here's what a radon test actually costs in Indianapolis, how the process works, and how to make sense of the number that comes back.

Radon almost always comes up during a real estate transaction rather than as a routine homeowner check-in, which means the test has to be scheduled and completed inside an already tight inspection contingency window. Building it into your very first inspection appointment, rather than as an afterthought once results from the rest of the report come back, keeps your timeline intact.

What a radon test costs

Bundled with a home inspection, a radon test typically runs $125–$175. As a standalone service scheduled separately, expect closer to $150–$225 since the technician has to make a dedicated trip to place and later retrieve the monitor. Bundling it with your full inspection is almost always the better value, and it means results come back on the same general timeline as the rest of your report.

How the test actually works

A continuous radon monitor is placed in the lowest livable level of the home — typically the basement — and left running for a minimum 48-hour period. The home needs "closed house conditions" during that window: windows shut, doors used normally, HVAC running as it normally would. Opening windows or running major exhaust fans during the test can invalidate the results, so if you're the seller and a test is underway, it's worth mentioning to whoever's still living there.

Reading your results

The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L. Results below that are considered acceptable, though the EPA notes there's no truly "safe" level and mitigation can still be worth considering between 2–4 pCi/L, especially for a household with children or anyone with existing respiratory concerns. Results above 4.0 pCi/L warrant a radon mitigation system, which typically costs $1,000–$1,500 installed and can often be negotiated into a purchase agreement as a seller-paid repair item.

Why Indianapolis-area homes test high so often

Most of Marion, Hamilton, Boone, Hendricks, and Johnson counties fall within EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest potential category, largely due to the region's underlying soil and glacial till. It's common for homes on opposite sides of the same street to test differently, since radon migrates through soil gas and enters through foundation cracks, sump pits, and slab penetrations — so a neighbor's clean test doesn't tell you much about your own home, and a prior test result from years ago shouldn't be assumed still accurate.

Should every buyer test?

Yes. Radon mitigation is one of the few inspection findings that's genuinely cheap to fix relative to the health risk of ignoring it, and testing adds only a modest amount to your total inspection invoice. It's one of the easiest add-ons to justify on a cost-benefit basis of any service we offer.

Key takeaways

  • A bundled radon test costs $125–$175; a standalone test runs $150–$225.
  • The test requires a continuous 48-hour monitoring period under closed-house conditions.
  • The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L, and most of central Indiana falls in Zone 1, the highest-risk category.
  • Mitigation systems typically cost $1,000–$1,500 and can often be negotiated as a seller-paid repair.
