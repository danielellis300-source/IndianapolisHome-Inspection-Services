How to Choose a Home Inspector in Indianapolis: 7 Red Flags to Avoid

March 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Since Indiana doesn't license home inspectors, the quality gap between a great inspector and a mediocre one can be wide — and you're the one responsible for telling the difference before you're the one holding the report. Here are the seven warning signs we tell every Indianapolis buyer to watch for.

1. No proof of insurance

Any inspector unwilling to show proof of E&O and general liability insurance is a hard pass, no exceptions. Without it, you have essentially no recourse if a major defect gets missed during the visit. This matters even more in Indiana than in licensed states, since there's no state board to fall back on either if something goes wrong.

2–3. Vague pricing and no sample report available

A legitimate inspector should be able to quote you a firm price based on square footage and requested add-ons before ever showing up to the property. If the price is suspiciously low compared to the going Indianapolis rate of $300–$500 for a standard home, ask directly what's being cut to hit that number — it's usually time on site or the level of detail in the report itself.

Along the same lines, every inspector worth hiring should be able to show you a sample report before you book, with no hesitation. If they can't or won't produce one, that's a strong signal the reports themselves are thin — a few checked boxes with no photos or narrative explanation of what was actually found and why it matters.

4. An agent who insists on only one inspector

It's completely normal for a real estate agent to recommend an inspector they've worked with before and trust. It's a problem if the agent insists on only that one inspector and actively discourages you from getting a second opinion or shopping around — that pattern can be a sign of a referral relationship that prioritizes a smooth, fast-closing transaction over a genuinely thorough report.

5. Discourages you from attending

A confident inspector wants you there. Being present lets you ask questions in real time and see issues firsthand rather than just reading about them later in a PDF. An inspector who actively discourages buyers from attending, or who schedules at a time they already know you can't make, is worth a second look before you commit to booking with them.

6. Slow, thin reports and poor follow-up

In the Indianapolis market, same-day digital reports with photos are the standard, not a premium add-on you should have to pay extra for. A report that takes a week and comes back as a bare checklist without photo documentation makes it much harder to use during negotiation, and harder to reference months later if an issue resurfaces. That same pattern often shows up again after the invoice is paid — a good inspector expects and welcomes follow-up questions for weeks after delivering the report, while a weaker one gets noticeably harder to reach. Check reviews specifically for mentions of post-inspection responsiveness, not just the inspection day itself.

Key takeaways

  • No proof of insurance and a suspiciously low price compared to the going $300–$500 rate are the two biggest red flags.
  • A legitimate inspector will always show you a sample report and welcome you attending the inspection in person.
  • Same-day, photo-rich digital reports are the local standard in Indianapolis, not a premium add-on.
  • Post-inspection responsiveness matters — check reviews specifically for how inspectors handle follow-up questions after the invoice is paid.
