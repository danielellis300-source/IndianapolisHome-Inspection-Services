New Construction Inspection Cost in Indianapolis: Why New Homes Still Need One

February 4, 2026 · 3 min read

New doesn't mean flawless. With Indianapolis suburbs like Westfield, Fishers, and Whitestown adding new subdivisions every year, we inspect newly built homes constantly — and defects are far more common than most buyers expect from a house nobody has ever lived in.

It's an easy trap to assume a brand-new home from a reputable builder doesn't need the same scrutiny as a resale property, especially when the builder's own team is already doing walkthroughs. In practice, new construction defects are often just as costly as resale issues — they're simply less expected, which makes catching them early even more valuable.

What it costs

A single final-walkthrough inspection on new construction typically runs $300–$450, roughly in line with a resale home of the same size. If you inspect at multiple construction phases — pre-drywall and final walkthrough, for example — expect to pay for each visit separately, usually $250–$350 per phase visit depending on how far along the home is.

The phase that matters most: pre-drywall

The single highest-value inspection on new construction happens before drywall goes up, when framing, electrical rough-in, plumbing rough-in, and HVAC ductwork are all still fully visible. Once walls are closed, correcting a framing or wiring issue means cutting into finished drywall to fix it — so catching it at this stage saves the builder, and by extension you, real money and hassle down the line.

Final walkthrough inspection

Before closing, a full inspection covers the same scope as a resale home: roof, attic, foundation, HVAC operation, electrical panel, plumbing fixtures, grading, and interior finish quality. It's common to find punch-list items — improperly sloped grading, missing insulation baffles, unsealed penetrations, or HVAC systems that were never properly balanced across rooms — that the builder's own quality walkthrough happened to miss.

Why the builder's inspector isn't a substitute

Builders typically run their own quality control walkthrough, but that inspector works for the builder, not you. An independent, buyer-hired inspector has no incentive to soften findings or rush through the punch list to hit a closing deadline. Most builder warranties also require defects to be reported within a specific window (often 30 days at close, then again near the one-year mark) — an independent report gives you clear paper documentation if a dispute comes up later over what was or wasn't disclosed.

The one-year warranty inspection

Many buyers skip this, and it's a mistake. Scheduling a follow-up inspection close to the one-year builder warranty deadline catches settling cracks, HVAC issues that only show up after a full heating and cooling season of real use, and drainage problems that only appear after real rain events — all while they're still the builder's responsibility to fix at no cost to you.

Key takeaways

  • A final walkthrough inspection on new construction runs $300–$450, similar to a resale home of the same size.
  • The pre-drywall phase is the single highest-value inspection, since framing and rough-in work is still fully visible.
  • A builder's own quality control walkthrough is not a substitute for an independent, buyer-hired inspection.
  • Scheduling a follow-up inspection near the one-year warranty deadline catches issues that only surface after a full season of use.
