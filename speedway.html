Home Inspections in Speedway, Indiana

A unique Marion County town built around the world's greatest race course

Speedway is one of the few incorporated towns in the country built almost entirely around a single institution: the Indianapolis Motor Speedway. Unlike the other communities on this list, Speedway is a legally independent municipality inside Marion County — it runs its own police and fire departments and its own public school corporation, separate from Indianapolis Public Schools. That independence shows up in the housing stock too: tight, walkable blocks of homes built from the 1920s through the 1960s to house track workers, race teams, and the auto-industry employees who once filled the manufacturing plants nearby.

The town is compact — just a few square miles bounded roughly by 16th Street, Crawfordsville Road, and the Speedway grounds themselves — so almost every home here is a short walk or drive from Main Street, the IMS Museum, and Gasoline Alley. Georgetown Road and Crawfordsville Road carry most of the through-traffic, while the residential streets in between stay quiet outside of race weekends. Despite the small-town feel, Speedway sits just inside I-465, so residents get a genuinely short commute into downtown Indianapolis.

Because most of Speedway's housing predates 1965, our inspectors see the same handful of issues again and again: original or early-replacement electrical panels that no longer meet modern load demands, galvanized supply lines that have been corroding from the inside for decades, and clay sewer laterals old enough to have root intrusion or bellied sections — the kind of problem a standard visual inspection can't catch but a sewer scope inspection can. Masonry block foundations are common too, and where a home has a crawl space rather than a full basement, moisture management deserves a close look — see our crawl space and basement inspection guide for what that actually involves.

Marion County as a whole carries elevated radon potential, and Speedway's mix of older masonry foundations is exactly the kind of construction where it's worth testing rather than guessing — our guide on whether you need a radon test in Indiana covers what that risk actually looks like locally. Whether you're buying your first home near the track or evaluating a rental a few blocks off Main Street, Indianapolis Home Inspection Services delivers the same-day, photo-documented report you need to move forward with confidence.
