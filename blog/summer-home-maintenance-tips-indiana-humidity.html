Summer Home Maintenance in Indiana: Fighting Humidity, Mold, and HVAC Overload

April 11, 2026 · 3 min read

Indiana summers bring a specific combination of heat and humidity that stresses HVAC systems and creates ideal conditions for mold growth in basements and crawl spaces. A little attention in June saves a lot of trouble, and a lot of money, by the time August rolls around.

Unlike winter maintenance, which is mostly about preventing a single catastrophic failure like a burst pipe, summer maintenance in Indiana is more about managing an ongoing condition — humidity — before it slowly turns into a mold or HVAC problem that's much more expensive to reverse than to prevent.

None of these steps require major investment — a dehumidifier, a filter change, and a few minutes of attention to ventilation habits go a long way toward preventing a much larger repair later.

Manage basement and crawl space humidity

Indoor relative humidity above roughly 60% starts to create conditions where mold can establish itself and spread, and Indianapolis summers regularly push outdoor humidity well past that threshold for weeks at a time. A dehumidifier in a finished or semi-finished basement is one of the single most effective preventive tools available, especially in homes without a fully conditioned basement space tied into the main HVAC system.

Give your AC system a mid-summer check

Replace or check the air filter monthly during peak cooling season — a clogged filter forces the whole system to work much harder and can lead to a frozen evaporator coil, which then makes cooling worse, not better. Clear leaves, grass clippings, and other debris away from the outdoor condenser unit, and make sure it has at least a couple feet of clearance on all sides for proper airflow through the coils.

Watch for condensation problems

Check ductwork located in unconditioned spaces like a crawl space or attic for condensation buildup, which can lead to moisture damage and mold if it drips consistently onto framing or insulation below it. Insulating exposed ductwork in these areas helps prevent the temperature differential that causes condensation to form on the duct surface in the first place.

Ventilate bathrooms and the kitchen properly

Run bathroom exhaust fans during and for a full 15–20 minutes after every shower, and confirm the kitchen range hood actually vents to the exterior rather than just recirculating air back into the room. These small daily habits meaningfully reduce the total interior moisture load a home has to deal with during Indiana's most humid stretch of the year.

Check the sump pump before storm season

Summer thunderstorms in central Indiana can dump a lot of rain in a very short window. Test your sump pump before storm season peaks in earnest, and consider a battery backup if you don't already have one installed — a power outage during a heavy storm is exactly when you need the pump working the most, and exactly when standard power is most likely to fail. If you're seeing ongoing moisture issues despite these steps, our mold inspection cost guide covers when it's worth bringing in a professional to test further.

Key takeaways

  • Indoor humidity above roughly 60% starts to create conditions where mold can establish itself and spread.
  • A basement dehumidifier is one of the most effective tools against Indiana's summer humidity load.
  • Replace AC filters monthly during peak season and keep at least two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser.
  • Test your sump pump, including any battery backup, before storm season peaks in central Indiana.
